# Round 3: the same rubric on a far denser scene

Package `starss23_iteration/case8-starss-scene-b`. The rounds 0-2 package
`starss23_iteration/case8-starss-single-delivery` is untouched; nothing frozen there was modified to build this

## Why the material changed instead of the rubric

| Round | Content /50 | Implementation /48 | Judge /2 | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| R0 corrected baseline | 34.61 | 48 | 1.5 | 84.11 |
| R1 performer tracking + 6 bookmarks | 34.82 | 46 | 1.5 | 82.32 |
| R2 revision workbench + 10 bookmarks | 31.30 | **48** | 1.5 | 80.80 |

Round 2 added eighteen points of genuinely stateful engineering: field editing with five validation rules, marker
dragging through the inverse projection, atomic undo and redo across composite gestures, derived-state and
bookmark-freshness invalidation, and an export/import/persistence round trip. A deliberately faulty artefact lost
5.48 of those points in exactly the right places. `gpt-6.1-sol` scored **18 out of 18**, and 48 out of 48 overall.

That is the finding, not a disappointment: this model does not fail at building the web product. It fails at
hearing the room. Every point of headroom is on the content side, so round 3 leaves the rubric completely alone
and changes the only thing that was ever the bottleneck — the recording.

## What changed

Only the scene. `fold4_room24_mix014`, original seconds 50 to 110, replaces `fold4_room15_mix008` 60 to 120.
Both are STARSS23 dev-test, both 60 seconds, 1920x960 equirectangular at 15fps, four-channel FOA at 24kHz, three
annotated agents, six classes present.

| | rounds 0-2 | round 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| simultaneous sources, mean | 1.32 | **3.33** |
| simultaneous sources, max | 2 | **5** |
| frames with 3 or more sources | **0%** | **77.8%** |
| frames with fewer than 2 sources | 68% | **0%** |
| event episodes | 26 | 36 |
| classes present | door, footsteps, instrument, laughter, speech, water | bell, clapping, domestic, laughter, music, speech |
| continuous whole-clip sources | none | **two** (domestic at azimuth -97, music at azimuth +45) |
| annotation direction jitter | 3.5 deg/frame | 0.9 deg/frame |

The window was chosen by scanning every dev-test clip whose video and audio are both retrievable, scoring each
60-second window by how many classes are substantially present, mean and peak polyphony, and annotation
stability, then discarding windows whose annotated direction is implausibly unstable. The current scene already
sat near the maximum of its own recording, so no window of `fold4_room15_mix008` was materially harder; the full
152-second span is not harder either, because 136 of those seconds are speech and the rare classes do not grow.

## What did not change

Weights, thresholds, tests, budgets and tools are byte-identical to round 2: content 50 as temporal macro F1 12,
joint class-and-location F1 at 25 degrees 12, segmentation-invariant episode coverage 8, cross-activity agent
IDF1 8, ten held-out bookmark answers 10; implementation 48 as the round-0 explorer 22, performer filter 2,
Follow 3, bookmark navigation 3, revision workbench 18; grounded LLM review 2. Ten model turns, 50 tool calls,
36 reference frames, 12 own screenshots, 20,000 output tokens, high reasoning, no evaluator feedback, no gold
access, empty project. `iteration/evaluate-r3.mjs` differs from `evaluate-r2.mjs` in two strings: the content
metric it calls and the version it records.

The ten bookmark questions are re-derived for this scene, because five of the round-2 questions name classes that
do not occur in it. Each answer is unique in the official annotation and the runner-up is far away.

One sentence was added to the task: sounds no person produces are attributed by the annotation to a single
non-tracked background agent even when they come from different directions. That is a disclosure of an annotation
convention the candidate could not otherwise guess, not an answer — this scene has three background sounds at
three different azimuths under one annotated source.

## Tool reproduction, checked rather than assumed

The shared perception cache had to be rebuilt for the new audio. Before doing that, the pipeline was re-run on the
rounds 0-2 audio and compared against the archived cache:

- YAMNet class patches: **124 of 124 identical**.
- FOA active-intensity directions: **identical**.
- ASR: 11 segments against the archived 14. Same model, same revision
  (`Systran/faster-whisper-base.en`, snapshot `3d3d5dee`), same decoding parameters; faster-whisper 1.2.1 merges
  and splits differently from the version used originally. Transcript content, 59.5s of coverage against 58.9s,
  and all 18 gold speech episodes touched in both. The characteristic failure the case documents — one
  over-long segment swallowing a silence — is present in both.

So the classifier and the direction estimator are bit-reproducible and the ASR is equivalent but not
bit-reproducible across library versions. This is disclosed rather than hidden.

One deliberate tool change for this scene: **the ASR VAD gate is disabled**. Silero VAD classifies this
music-dominated mix as entirely non-speech and returns zero segments, which would have handed the candidate a
strictly weaker tool stack than earlier rounds had and made any score drop unattributable. With the gate off the
same model returns 27 segments covering 40.1 seconds, comparably noisy to the earlier scene's 14 over 58.9
seconds. Only the upstream gate changed; the model, revision and decoding parameters did not.

## Base evaluator correction for whole-clip events

The round-0 marker check probes each event at a moment when it should be inactive and requires the marker to be
hidden. Two events in this scene are audible for the entire 60 seconds, so no such moment exists and the probe
could never pass — it would have penalised a candidate precisely for correctly annotating continuous background,
which is the behaviour this scene is meant to reward. In the scene-B copy of
`evaluator/evaluate-ui-compatible.mjs`, an event spanning the whole clip is marked not applicable for that
sub-check instead of failed. The rounds 0-2 file is untouched.

The ratio is right and the waveform is not — exactly the counterfeit the repair was built from, now observed in
a genuine candidate rather than a synthetic one. The raw decoder check is worth 9 in the 48-point base evaluator. R3 scales that entire base bucket to 22, so changing only this check changes the R3 total by 9 * 22/48 = 4.125 points, not 9.

## Third candidate, and the output cap that had to go

`claude-opus-5` was added as a third candidate on the identical frozen task, at reasoning effort `xhigh` on
request. Two protocol facts had to be fixed first, both recorded in `iteration/round-3/`:

**Reasoning effort was hard-coded.** `runner/gateway.mjs` sent `reasoning: {effort: 'high'}` regardless of what
the protocol declared. It now reads `OMNIALE_REASONING_EFFORT`, still defaults to `high`, and records the effort
actually used in `trace.json` under `transport.reasoning_effort`. The two GPT candidates ran at `high`; Opus 5
ran at `xhigh`. That is a disclosed difference in reasoning budget, not an equal-effort comparison.

**The per-response output cap was a confound.** `budgets.max_output_tokens` was 20,000 in every round and was
applied to every candidate, so it looked neutral. It was not. Measured maxima for a single response:

| Candidate | largest single response | truncated by the cap |
|---|---:|---:|
| gpt-6.1-sol, round 3 | 11,428 | 0 |
| gpt-6-luna, round 3 | 7,408 | 0 |
| gpt-6.1-sol, round 2 | 7,608 | 0 |
| claude-opus-5, round 3, xhigh | **20,000** | **2** |

Reasoning tokens count against the same cap, so at `xhigh` Opus 5 spent the whole allowance on turns 4 and 5,
emitted reasoning with no tool call, and delivered no files at all. A cap that binds one candidate and not
another measures the cap, not the candidate.

The cap is therefore **removed**, along with the request deadline, and `budgets.max_output_tokens` and
`request_timeout_ms` are now `null`: no candidate is cut off mid-answer. The turn (10), tool-call (50),
reference-frame (36) and screenshot (12) budgets are untouched — those are the task.

This does not disturb the existing results. No GPT response ever reached the old cap or returned status
`incomplete`, so the cap was never applied to them as a constraint and the recorded 70.09 and 47.79 stand without
a re-run. The truncated attempt is kept as `runs/opus5-agentic-r3-01` as evidence and must not be read as a
capability result; the scored run is `runs/opus5-agentic-r3-05`.

## The arithmetic to be honest about

Implementation is 48 points and `gpt-6.1-sol` has now scored 46, 48 and 48 on it. Luna's 39.81 shows the
component still separates a weaker candidate from a stronger one; what it no longer does is separate a strong
candidate from a perfect one. While that holds, a total below 70
with the judge included requires content below 20 out of 50, that is below 40 percent, and the ablation above puts
"speech only" at 17.47. The model scored 62.6 percent of content on the easy scene. That is what happened: content fell to 20.59, which is 41 percent, and the
total landed at 70.09. The scene change moved the score 10.71 points in one round and every point of it came from
the content side, but it stopped essentially exactly at the boundary the arithmetic predicts.

Reaching the target would then require changing what the rubric counts — retiring or gating a component every
candidate maxes out. That is a defensible benchmark-design decision and it is also exactly the kind of
re-weighting that must not be used to manufacture a target. It is reported here as a decision for the owner of
the benchmark, with the measurements to support it, rather than taken quietly.
